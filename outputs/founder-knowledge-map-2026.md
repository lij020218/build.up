## 1. Legal & Administrative

### 1.1 Incorporation Decision: Individual vs Corporate

**What founders need to decide FIRST:**
- 개인사업자 vs 법인사업자 — impacts taxes, liability, investment eligibility
- AI/tech startups: 법인 strongly recommended for investment eligibility and government program access
- Cost: Incorporation typically 30-50만원 via services like 헬프미(help-me.kr)
- Timeline: 2-4 weeks for full incorporation including bank account

**Decision framework:**
| Factor | 개인사업자 | 법인(주식회사) |
|--------|-----------|--------------|
| Setup cost | Nearly free | 30-50만원 |
| Tax rate | 6-45% progressive | 9-24% corporate |
| Investment eligible | Very limited | Yes (required for VC) |
| Gov program eligible | Some | Most (required for 초기창업패키지 etc.) |
| Credibility | Low | High |
| Liability | Unlimited personal | Limited to capital |

**Where founders currently search:** 헬프미 블로그, k-startbiz.org, 네이버 블로그, 세무사 유튜브
**Fragmentation level:** HIGH -- information is scattered and often outdated

### 1.2 Essential Contracts Checklist

**6 contracts every startup MUST have before hiring:**

1. **임원계약서 (Executive Agreement)** -- defines roles, responsibilities, non-compete, confidentiality for co-founders/executives
2. **정규직 근로계약서 + 개인정보제공동의서** -- legally mandatory; failure to provide = 과태료 (fine)
3. **계약직 근로계약서 + 개인정보제공동의서** -- for 1-2 year term employees
4. **단기간근로 근로계약서** -- for part-time/event staff (팝업스토어, 박람회)
5. **프리랜서 용역계약서** -- result-based delivery contracts; critical distinction from employment
6. **비밀유지 서약서 (NDA/Confidentiality)** -- protects trade secrets, essential before sharing with partners

**Additional contracts by stage:**

| Stage | Contract Needed |
|-------|----------------|
| Co-founding | 주주간계약서 (Shareholders' Agreement) via ZUZU template |
| Customer-facing | 서비스 이용약관, 개인정보처리방침 |
| B2B sales | SLA, 거래기본계약서 |
| Fundraising | 투자계약서 (Term Sheet → SHA → SPA) |
| IP creation | 직무발명보상규정, 저작권 양도계약 |

**Sources:** [넥스트유니콘](https://www.nextunicorn.kr/insight/b804d0818cf92165), [ZUZU 주주간계약서 가이드](https://zuzu.network/resource/blog/how-to-write-founders-agreement/), [k-startbiz.org](https://blog.k-startbiz.org)

### 1.3 Privacy Policy & Terms of Service (2025 Revision)

**Critical 2025 update (applies to 2026 services):**

The 개인정보보호위원회 revised the 개인정보 처리방침 작성지침 in April 2025:

- **Consent simplification:** Services no longer need consent for collecting data necessary for contract fulfillment
- **Minors (14세 미만):** Now a MANDATORY section -- must specify guardian consent acquisition method
- **Mobile optimization:** Privacy policy can be displayed in settings/menu, not just homepage
- **Behavioral data:** Specific templates provided for 맞춤형 광고 (targeted advertising) disclosure
- **Penalty:** Up to 3% of global annual revenue for non-compliance

**Required documents for ANY online service:**
1. 개인정보처리방침 (Privacy Policy) -- per 개인정보보호법 Article 30
2. 서비스 이용약관 (Terms of Service)
3. 개인정보 수집/이용 동의서 (if collecting beyond contract necessity)
4. 마케팅 수신 동의 (separate opt-in required)

### 1.4 IP Protection Strategy

**Patent vs Trade Secret decision framework:**

| Factor | Patent (특허) | Trade Secret (영업비밀) |
|--------|-------------|----------------------|
| Protection period | 20 years (registered country only) | Unlimited (if maintained) |
| Content disclosure | Required (public) | No disclosure needed |
| Cost | Registration + maintenance fees | Zero (but management cost) |
| Best for | Novel algorithms, hardware, processes | Customer data, business methods, pricing models |
| Risk | Competitors can design around | Lost if leaked |

**2025 지식재산경영 전략 가이드북** recommends IP-MIX strategy: combine patents for core tech + trade secrets for implementation details.

**Free IP support for startups:**
- 특허청 IP-R&D 전략지원 (free patent search + strategy)
- 서울지식재산센터 가이드북 (Seoul-based startups)
- K-Startup 지식재산 멘토링

### 1.5 Stock Options (스톡옵션) Tax Optimization

**Critical timing rule:** Venture certification (벤처기업 인증) MUST be obtained BEFORE granting stock options to receive tax benefits.

**Three tax benefits for 벤처기업:**

1. **비과세 특례:** Up to 2억원/year (cumulative 5억원) tax-exempt on exercise gains
2. **분할 납부:** If gains exceed 2억원, spread tax payment over 5 years
3. **과세 이연:** Choose to defer taxation to time of share sale (양도소득세 10-30% vs 종합소득세 6-45%)

**2026 upcoming change:** 중소벤처기업부 is expanding the stock option limit to 5억원 per employee (시행령 개정 예정, expected H2 2026).

**Where founders currently search:** ZUZU guides, 세무법인 가치, 삼쩜삼, 마일스톤 블로그, 세움택스
**Fragmentation:** VERY HIGH -- tax implications are complex and spread across accounting firm blogs

**Korea vs Silicon Valley Investment Differences:**

| Aspect | Korea | Silicon Valley |
|--------|-------|---------------|
| Preferred instrument | RCPS (상환전환우선주) | SAFE → CPS (전환우선주) |

**SAFE in Korea (legal basis: 벤처투자법 제2조 제1호 라목):**
- Requires ALL shareholder consent (vs US: no consent needed)
- Korean SAFE includes personal liability for founders/representatives
- Must notify new investors of existing SAFE agreements in writing

### 3.3 Freelancer vs Full-Time Decision Framework

**When to hire freelancers (recommended for early-stage):**
- Tasks that don't require daily full-time work
- Rapidly changing priorities requiring flexible resource allocation
- Specialist skills needed temporarily (design sprints, launch campaigns)
- Matching possible within 1 week vs 2-3 months for full-time
- Cost: Pay only for deliverables, no 4대보험 obligations

**When to hire full-time:**
- Core product development (continuous iteration)
- Roles requiring deep company knowledge accumulation
- When monthly work consistently exceeds 160 hours
- Customer-facing roles requiring brand consistency

**Key legal distinction:** Freelancers must be genuinely independent (control their own schedule/methods). If you treat them like employees, courts may reclassify them -- leading to back-pay for 4대보험, 퇴직금, etc.

## 4. Product & Technical Decisions

### 4.1 MVP Scope Decision Framework (2026)

**The "One Workflow" Rule:**
- MVP scope fails because teams ship features, not a usable workflow
- A well-scoped MVP: 3-5 core features solving the PRIMARY problem
- If timeline exceeds 4 months, you're building too much
- Simple MVPs: 6-8 weeks | Complex (custom infra): 12-16 weeks max

**What to include:**
- Core value proposition workflow (the one thing that proves your hypothesis)
- Basic user authentication
- Minimal but functional UI/UX
- Essential analytics to measure success
- Feedback collection mechanism

**What to cut:**
- Admin dashboards (use direct DB queries initially)
- Multi-platform (start web-only or mobile-only)
- Edge cases (handle manually until scale demands automation)
- Perfect design (functional > beautiful at MVP stage)
- Multi-language support (pick your primary market)

### 4.2 Build vs Buy vs Partner Framework (2026)

**Decision matrix:**
| Criteria | Build | Buy (SaaS) | Partner |
|----------|-------|-----------|---------|
| Time to market | Slow (months) | Fast (days) | Medium (weeks) |
| Cost upfront | High (dev time) | Low (subscription) | Variable |
| Customization | Full | Limited | Negotiable |
| Best when | Core differentiator | Commodity function | Strategic capability |
| Example | Your AI algorithm | Auth (Supabase), Email (Resend) | Payment (Stripe/Toss) |

**Rule of thumb:** Build only what is your competitive advantage. Buy everything else.

### 4.4 Security Baseline Checklist (Pre-Series A)

**51 must-haves organized by category:**

**Authentication & Access (8 items):** MFA for all admin, SSO enforcement, least privilege IAM, quarterly service account rotation, secrets manager (not env vars in git), session expiry, password complexity

**Data Protection (7 items):** Encryption at rest + in transit (TLS 1.2+), PII classification, encrypted backups tested regularly, data retention policy, GDPR/PIPA compliance documentation, third-party data sharing review

**Application Security (8 items):** OWASP Top 10 addressed, input validation, HTTPS + HSTS, CSP headers, dependency auditing (Dependabot/Snyk), rate limiting, file upload validation, no stack traces in production

**AI/LLM Security (8 items):** Prompt injection protection, output validation, explicit consent for data sent to LLM APIs, data residency compliance, PII redaction before API calls, scoped API keys, per-user rate limits, audit logging

**Infrastructure (8 items):** No direct public internet access to production, security group documentation, CI/CD vulnerability scanning, code review for deployments, audit logs, automated backups with tested restore, DDoS protection, infrastructure as code

**Incident Response (6 items):** Documented IR plan, security monitoring/alerting, on-call rotation, breach notification process, bug bounty/responsible disclosure page, post-incident review process

**SOC 2 Readiness (6 items):** Scope defined, compliance automation (Vanta/Drata), controls mapped, employee security training, background checks, vendor risk assessments

## 5. Marketing & Growth (Pre-revenue)

### 5.1 Product Hunt Launch Playbook (2026)

**Pre-Launch (4-6 weeks before):**
1. Build seed audience of 20-50 genuine supporters (quality > quantity)
2. Create waitlist with teaser page
3. Grow newsletter list (Dub had 25,000+ subscribers at launch)
4. If open-source, leverage GitHub community

**Launch Assets Preparation (1-2 weeks):**
- High-quality product carousel images (more important than video!)
- Craft tagline: "clear beats clever" -- avoid buzzwords
- Write a compelling First Comment sharing personal motivation
- Create memorable short link (e.g., d.to/ph)
- Deploy Teaser page 1 week before (can trend in "Coming Soon")

**Launch Day (12:01 AM Pacific Time):**
- Best days: Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday
- Avoid Monday (high competition), Friday (low traffic)
- Self-submit (hunters no longer provide advantage)
- Send announcement email simultaneously
- Post personal threads on X and LinkedIn (direct linking is fine)
- Reply to EVERY Product Hunt comment
- Use Hunted Space to track votes (hidden for first 4 hours)
- Update website with launch popup, GitHub README with banner

**Post-Launch:**
- Treat as distribution event, not vanity metric
- Convert spike into 30-day monetization system
- The goal: retained users, not upvote count

**Dub.co results:** 1,085 upvotes, #1 Product of Day/Week/Month, 663 signups (8x daily), 2,000+ launch-day visitors

## 6. Operations & Compliance

### 6.1 개인정보보호법 (PIPA) Compliance

**2025 Amendment Key Points (applies to all 2026 services):**

- **Penalties:** Up to 3% of global annual revenue
- **Domestic representative:** Foreign entities must designate one (expanded role in Oct 2025 amendment)
- **AI/Generative AI:** PIPC released 4-stage lifecycle guidelines (purpose setting -> strategy -> training -> management)
- **Data breach:** Must notify both data subjects AND PIPC

**Minimum compliance checklist for startups:**
1. Write and publish 개인정보처리방침 following 2025 작성지침
2. Appoint 개인정보보호책임자 (can be CEO at early stage)
3. Implement data encryption (at rest + in transit)
4. Establish data retention/deletion policy
5. Create breach notification procedure
6. If using AI: comply with PIPC generative AI guidelines
7. If targeting minors: additional guardian consent mechanisms

### 6.2 ISMS/ISMS-P Certification

**When it becomes mandatory:**
- 정보통신 서비스 매출액 100억원 이상
- 이용자 수 100만명 이상
- Or: 의료기관, 교육기관, etc.

**For startups below thresholds:**
- 간편 인증 available for SMEs (매출 300억 미만)
- Reduced requirements and costs
- Deadline for first-time mandatory applicants: August 31 of the following year

**Process:** 신청 -> 계약 (수수료 산정) -> 심사 -> 인증

**Source:** [KISA ISMS-P](https://isms.kisa.or.kr), [엑소스피어](https://exosp.com/blog/isms-p-simple-certification)

### 6.3 Insurance Needs for Startups

**Recommended insurance types:**
1. **D&O (임원배상책임보험):** Protects directors from personal liability in lawsuits -- increasingly important as AI and regulatory risks grow in 2026
2. **일반배상책임보험:** Covers third-party bodily injury or property damage
3. **사이버보험:** Covers data breach costs, ransomware, business interruption
4. **고용관련 보험:** Beyond mandatory 4대보험, covers wrongful termination claims
5. **기업휴지보험:** Business interruption coverage

**2026 D&O market:** Prices stabilizing after soft market; AI governance and financial distress are emerging risk areas that insurers scrutinize heavily.

## 7. Mental Health & Founder Wellbeing

### 7.1 The Hidden Crisis (2025-2026 Data)

**Alarming statistics:**
- 73% of tech founders report persistent burnout symptoms for 3+ months (2025 CA survey)
- 54% experienced burnout in past 12 months
- 46% rate their mental health as "bad" or "very bad"
- 75% have experienced anxiety
- 61% suffer sleep disruptions
- 2/3 of founders HIDE mental health struggles from investors and co-founders

**"Shadow burnout"** -- founders meeting/exceeding business targets while burning out internally -- is identified as the most dangerous form because it's invisible.

**Primary barrier to seeking help:** Fear that therapy records will surface during investor due diligence, acquisition, or board evaluations.

### 7.2 Resources and Strategies

**Professional resources:**
- Jerry Colonna / Reboot (executive coaching for founders)
- Dr. Michael Freeman (researcher on entrepreneur mental health)
- VC-provided therapy programs (ask your investors -- funds that provide access see better startup performance)

**Practical strategies:**
- Peer support networks (founder groups, YPO, EO)
- Structured work boundaries (no-meeting days, digital detox periods)
- Regular exercise and sleep hygiene
- Delegation -- "if it's not your 10x activity, delegate it"
- Journaling and reflection practices
- Professional coaching/therapy (normalize it)

**What build.up could offer:**
- Anonymous founder wellness check-ins (periodic mood/stress surveys)
- Curated list of Korean founder support groups
- Mental health resource directory (Korean therapists who understand startup context)

## 9. International Expansion

### 9.1 Why Korean Startups Go Global Early

- Korea's domestic market is limited -- many startups plan global from Day 1
- Investors increasingly want to see global potential
- Key requirements for international investors:
  - English pitch deck
  - International accounting standards (K-IFRS or US GAAP)
  - Global market data in business plans

### 9.2 Delaware C-Corp Setup

**When to consider:**
- Planning to raise from US investors
- Targeting US market directly
- Need US entity for partnerships/contracts

**Key decisions:**
- LLC vs C-Corporation: VCs strongly prefer C-Corp
- Delaware: Most common jurisdiction (favorable corporate law, Court of Chancery)
- Options: DIY ($500-1,500) vs Agency ($3,000-8,000+)
- Need: Registered Agent in Delaware, EIN from IRS, US bank account

**Challenge for Korean founders:**
- RCPS structure from Korean investors is difficult to reconcile with US CPS structure
- "플립" (Flip) -- restructuring Korean entity under US holding company -- requires careful planning
- Tax implications in both countries need specialist advice
